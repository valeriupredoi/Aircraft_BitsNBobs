## A-4M BuNo 159486

Nicely painted A-4M 159486 on the VF-126 line at NAS Miramar, December 1992. Aircraft was sent to Argentina in November 1995.

**159486 has a very similar story to 160045 below, but it ended up as Argentinian C-934 rather than C-922.**

### Douglas A-4M Skyhawk II — BuNo 159486

**c/n 145?**
**Argentine FAA:** **C-934**

159486 was one of the **24 A-4Ms in the BuNo 159470–159493 production block**, so it's an early-production A-4M. ([Skyhawk Association][1])

#### USMC career

* **1975–79 — VMA-223 "Bulldogs"**, **WP-16**

  * It was already with the Bulldogs by 1976 and was photographed at **RAAF Amberley in August 1977** as WP-16. ([Skyhawk Association][2])
  * There are also photographs of it with **Lt Ron Schlitt** in September 1978. ([Skyhawk Association][2])

* **1980–81 — VMA-331 "Bumblebees"**, **VL-14**

* **1981 — returned to VMA-223**, becoming **WP-14**

* **1982–83 — VMA-223**

* **1983 onward — VMA-322 "Fighting Gamecocks"**, initially **QR-65**

  * It remained with VMA-322 through the 1980s. ([Forgotten Jets][3])

* **1992–94 — VF-126 "Bandits"**, **NJ-632**

  * This is an interesting late-career assignment: VF-126 was a Navy adversary squadron rather than a normal Marine attack squadron.
  * By this point the Skyhawk was wearing the **blue/grey adversary scheme** seen on several late A-4Ms.

* **March 1994 — AMARC**

  * Entered the Davis-Monthan storage facility as **3A0745**. The Skyhawk Association specifically identifies 159486 as AMARC 3A0745 and has a 1994 photograph of it there. ([Skyhawk Association][4])

### Argentina 🇦🇷

Then, in **November 1995**, 159486 was sold to Argentina for the **A-4AR Fightinghawk programme**.

It became:

> **FAA C-934 — A-4AR Fightinghawk**

and joined **Grupo 5 de Caza**. ([Skyhawk Association][5])

The A-4AR conversion gave these old A-4Ms a *seriously* modernised cockpit and avionics suite, including the **AN/APG-66V2 (ARG-1) radar**, HOTAS, HUD, new mission computers, INS and electronic-warfare equipment. ([Skyhawk Association][6])

### So its lineage is:

**A-4M 159486**
↓
**VMA-223 — WP-16**
↓
**VMA-331 — VL-14**
↓
**VMA-223 — WP-14**
↓
**VMA-322 — QR-65**
↓
**VF-126 — NJ-632**
↓
**AMARC 3A0745**
↓
**Argentina — A-4AR C-934**
↓
**Grupo 5 de Caza**

And there's a nice modelling/history connection here: **C-934 is one of the Argentine A-4ARs whose original identity is particularly easy to establish because the BuNo → FAA serial chain is explicitly documented as 159486 → C-934.** ([Skyhawk Association][5])

## A-4M BuNo 160045

Top Gun A-4M BuNo 160045, NFWS-50, on the Miramar ramp in a brown/tan camouflage scheme, 1992-1994. Name below the canopy is CAPT Arrett, call sign Badger.

**BuNo 160045 is quite an interesting Skyhawk**, because it went from a late USMC A-4M to becoming the **first A-4AR Fightinghawk airframe completed in Argentina**.

### Douglas A-4M Skyhawk II — BuNo 160045

**c/n 14547**

* **Built:** 1976/77 as an A-4M for the US Marine Corps. It was part of the final production batch of 24 A-4Ms numbered **160022–160045**. ([Skyhawk Association][1])
* **1977–80:** Served with **VMA-211 "Wake Island Avengers"**, initially carrying the **CF-1** modex. There are contemporary photos of 160045 as CF-1. ([Skyhawk Association][2])
* **1980–81:** **VMA-214 "Black Sheep"**.
* **1981:** Moved through **VMA-331** as **VL-18**, then **VMA-223 "Bulldogs"** as **WP-18**.
* **1982:** VMA-223 and then VMA-331.
* **1982–84:** **VMA-322 "Fighting Gamecocks"**.
* **1984–88:** VMA-322, principally as **QR-62**. ([Skyhawk Association][3])
* **Late 1980s:** Associated with **VX-5** for weapons/evaluation work.
* **1992–94:** Fighter Weapons School at **MCAS Miramar**, carrying **"50"**. ([Joe Baugher][4])
* **17 May 1994:** Entered **AMARC** at Davis-Monthan as **3A0773**. ([Joe Baugher][4])

### Then came Argentina 🇦🇷

In **August 1995**, 160045 was sold to Argentina as part of the A-4M fleet selected for conversion into the **A-4AR Fightinghawk**. It became **FAA C-922**, Grupo 5 de Caza. ([Joe Baugher][4])

And this is the particularly cool bit:

**C-922/160045 was the first A-4AR airframe actually worked on and completed at LMAASA in Córdoba.** Its official roll-out was **3 August 1998**, making it effectively the first aircraft of the new Argentine A-4AR fleet. ([historiasdeaviones.blogspot.com][5])

The conversion was considerably more than a cosmetic rebuild. The A-4Ms received a new digital avionics suite, including the **AN/APG-66V2 radar**, HUD, HOTAS, mission computers, INS, radar warning/jamming equipment and other systems. ([Wikipedia][6])

So the lineage is:

**A-4M 160045 → USMC Skyhawk → AMARC 3A0773 → Argentine C-922 → first A-4AR Fightinghawk completed in Argentina.**

One little caveat: the detailed USMC assignment dates differ slightly between the various Skyhawk databases, but **the overall 160045 → C-922 identity is very solid**, and the Argentine source specifically identifies it as the first LMAASA A-4AR. ([historiasdeaviones.blogspot.com][5])
