I found the **actual encounter report**, and it clears up a few things from my previous answer. In particular, Gossard's victory was much more specific than I had initially been able to establish.

### Gossard's combat on 5 December 1944

The surviving USAAF encounter report gives:

* **1st Lt. Philip D. Gossard**
* **435th Fighter Squadron, 479th Fighter Group**
* P-51 Mustang
* **5 December 1944**
* **1100 hrs**
* **Northwest of Berlin, Germany**
* German force: a mixed formation of **Fw 190s and Bf 109s**
* **One Fw 190 destroyed**. ([8th Air Force][1])

And the report is wonderfully terse:

> "A gaggle of about 40 FW-190's and Me 109's mixed was sighted..."

Gossard was below/behind the formation and picked out an Fw 190 on the **outside rear of the formation's turn**. He closed to approximately **50 yards**, fired a short burst, and the German aircraft **"blew up immediately."** He claimed one Fw 190 destroyed, with his claim certified by **2nd Lt. Larry J. Cano**. ([8th Air Force][1])

So we can now say with confidence that his official victory was an **Fw 190**, not merely an unspecified German fighter.

### What was happening around him?

This was a **major fighter interception over the Berlin area**, and Gossard was not operating in isolation.

Other 479th pilots' reports from the same engagement are extraordinarily useful for reconstructing what happened.

Major **Arthur Jeffrey**, leading a nine-P-51 section of the 434th FS, encountered approximately **40 Fw 190s with about 15 Bf 109s above them** near **Neuruppin, about 10 miles northwest of Berlin**, at roughly 26–27,000 ft. The Germans were apparently positioning themselves to attack the bomber formation. Jeffrey led his P-51s into the attack. ([8th Air Force][2])

The 435th FS reports from **Gossard, Candelaria and Doolittle** describe essentially the same engagement at around **1100 hrs**, northwest of Berlin. Candelaria describes his flight seeing the German formation about 4,000 ft below them and turning into it; Doolittle likewise describes the large Fw 190 formation. ([8th Air Force][3])

So Gossard's encounter was almost certainly part of **one large swirling engagement involving several 479th FG formations**, rather than a separate little dogfight.

And the results were impressive. The 479th claimed a considerable number of German fighters during the action. Jeffrey's official citation later stated that his section alone destroyed **seven enemy aircraft**, with additional probable/damaged aircraft. ([Hall of Valor][4])

### Gossard's position in the action

His own description gives a nice picture of how he got his kill:

**German formation → ~40 Fw 190/Bf 109 → slow climbing right turn → Gossard picks an aircraft on outside rear → closes to ~50 yd → short burst → Fw 190 explodes.**

That's actually quite interesting tactically. He wasn't making a long-range head-on attack; he got **very close**, presumably because he was able to exploit the German formation's turn and attack an aircraft which was temporarily disadvantaged.

And the official USAAF victory database independently confirms the result:

**GOSSARD, PHILIP D., 1st Lt., 435th Fighter Squadron — 1.00 aerial victory — 5 December 1944.** His service number is given as **A-740218**. ([DAF History][5])

So there's no ambiguity here: **Gossard really did shoot down an Fw 190 on 5 December 1944.**

## And now the interesting bit: *V for VAL*

There's a correction to my previous answer here.

The P-51 associated with Gossard was:

**P-51D-10-NA, serial 44-14354, "V for VAL".**

Eduard's research identifies it specifically as Gossard's Mustang, assigned to the 435th FS at Wattisham. ([Info EDUARD][6])

But the aircraft's fate is a little different from what I implied previously.

**44-14354 was not damaged in combat on 2 December. It suffered a ground-loop accident at RAF Wattisham on 2 December 1944.** The surviving serial-number records describe it as badly damaged, with Gossard surviving. ([cgibin.rcn.com][7])

That's important because it means that **Gossard's 5 December victory cannot have been flown in 44-14354**.

In other words:

**2 Dec 1944 — 44-14354 "V for VAL" — ground-looped at Wattisham**

**5 Dec 1944 — Gossard — flew another 435th FS Mustang — shot down an Fw 190 northwest of Berlin**

That's a much more interesting story than the simple sequence I gave you earlier.

Eduard says that *V for VAL* was withdrawn after the accident and that Gossard's operational tour ended a few days later. ([Info EDUARD][6])

### And Gossard was there from the beginning

There's another nice detail from John Stanaway's history of the 479th.

Gossard had joined the **435th FS in January 1944**, while the group was still training in California on P-38 Lightnings. Stanaway specifically mentions **George Sykes and Phil Gossard** among the pilots posted to the 435th at that time. ([Scribd][8])

The 479th subsequently went to England and operated from **RAF Wattisham**, initially with P-38s and converting to P-51s during September–October 1944. ([8th Air Force][9])

So Gossard wasn't a late replacement who arrived shortly before his victory. He appears to have been a **fairly long-serving original 435th FS pilot**, who went through the group's P-38 training, deployment to England, transition to Mustangs and then combat.

That's quite a nice little career arc.
