On 3 April 2026, an A-10C Thunderbolt II (Warthog) 78-0614 MSN A10-0234 nicknamed "Toad", assigned to 23rd Operations Support Squadron (23rd OSS, 23 FG), part of the 23rd Wing based at Moody Air Force Base, Georgia, crashed off the coast of Musandam, Oman (in the Strait of Hormuz), with its polot rescued. It is believed that the A-10 was flying a Sandy mission, providing support for the rescue operations (CSAR - combat search and rescue) aimed at rescuing the crew of DUDE 44, the RAF Lakenheath F-15E Strike Eagle shot down by Iranian forces over the Zagros Mountains south of Isfahan. The Warthog pilot successfully flew the crippled aircraft out of Iranian airspace before ejecting safely over the Persian Gulf near Kuwait. Alongside the loss of DUDE 44 earlier that same day, the shootdown marked the first combat loss of an A-10 since the 2003 invasion of Iraq and made April 3 the worst single-day manned aircraft loss of Operation Epic Fury.

On September 24, 2025, a package of FT-coded aircraft from the 74th and 75th FS departed Moody AFB, transiting through Portsmouth International Airport and Naval Station Rota. By October 2025, 12 of these aircraft, including 78-0583, 78-0613, 78-0614, 79-0095, 79-0157, 80-0188, 80-0243, 80-0273, and 81-0988, had arrived in the Middle East. As Operation Epic Fury intensified and combat losses occurred, the fleet was heavily reinforced.

Though the Pentagon has not yet officially released any information about 78-0614 (pilot name, call sign, even tail number), it is now almost certain that "Toad" was the aircraft that was lost: on 13 June 2026 all the Moody A-10s transited via RAF Lakenheath, during their return flight back to the US, and "Toad" was the only one missing.
