## F-16D 90-0778

90-0778, MSI/C/N 1D-56, is a Block 42H F-16D with the small NSI air intake, and Pratt-Whitney F100 turbofan. On December 27, 1992, piloted by Lt Col Gary "Nordo" North, callsign BENJI41, it shot down an Iraqi MiG-25 Foxbat that had crossed the 32nd parallel (32N), the demarcation line for the no-fly zone imposed during Operation Southern Watch (a no-fly zone meant to protect the Iraqi Shiia population from Saddam's reprisals, in the wake of the brutal suppression of the 1991 Shiia Uprising). The aircraft belonged to 19th FS, 363 FW, but it was on loan to the 33rd FS, which was deployed to Dharan AB, Saudi Arabia. The kill marked the first ever kill by an AIM-120 AMRAAM, and the first kill of a USAF F-16. As of the latest records, 90-0778 is still flying in active duty with the USAF 310 FS "Tophats" of the 56th Fighter Wing at Luke AFB, Arizona, having served with the 310th Fighter Squadron (and previously the 308th FS). The current scheme, applied in 2022, is an homage to the December 1992 mission; the aircraft is painted in a USMC sand (FS33711)-brown (FS33448)-neutral grey (FS36270) - colours provided from the Caracal CD48232 decal set, The paint scheme was based on the experimental scheme applied to 84-1212 in 1990, and bears the markings of the 56th Fighter Wing (tail code LN, though, during December 1992 it was wearing the standard grey camouflage, with tail code EN). As for the Iraqi MiG-25, and her pilot - not much is known, the aircraft appears to be a PDS/PD interceptor MiG-25, with her pilot having not survived the shoot-down.

Crew Chief Roy Murray launched the aircraft that day, saying: "On 27 Dec 1992 I launched "Benji 41" for a combat mission from Dhahran, Saudi Arabia. The only reason for it flying the combat mission (4 Hours) that day was to take flying hours off it so it could go in for a phase inspection. Most of its flight time during Operation Southern Watch was (FAM) rides for in coming pilots and VIP's, being the only D model. The canopy was so bad that Lt. Col. North (at that time commander of the 33rd FS) stated "it was like landing with a paper bag on your head. HA! The family bus still got the job done that day. I also remember a reporter asking Lt. Col. North why he did not use the cheaper missile, his come back was great; he stated if you were in a fight and had a knife and a gun, which one would you use. We painted the star and a blue tail on it while it was in phase, because it was on loan from the 19th FS at the time, then it became a 33rd FS aircraft. I stayed as the Crew Chief of 778 when we returned to Shaw, AFB and then transferred out with it to Luke AFB, AZ. I took my retirement flight in 90-0778 and launched Gen. North one more time when he came through Luke. I do miss those days!"
