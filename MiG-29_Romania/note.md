## MiG-29 in Romanian Air Force

Romania's MiG-29 story is one of the great "what ifs" of post-Cold War European aviation. The Fulcrum was, by a comfortable margin, the most capable fighter the Romanian Air Force ever operated until the arrival of the F-16, yet it served for only about 13 years and never fulfilled its potential.

The negotiations between Romania and the Soviet Union regarding the purchase of a number of MiG-29s were started in the middle of 1987. Romania intended to replace the MiG-21MF with the new MiG-29, so, initially, 45 airframes were ordered, but only 21 were delivered between 1989-1990. The order for the remaining 24 aircraft was later cancelled. The first four airframes arrived in Romania, at Mihail Kogalniceanu, on 21 December 1989, the other 17 entering operational service the following year. All 21 aircraft – 17 single-seater MiG-29A 9-12A and four two-seater MiG-29UB 9.51 – were deployed with the 2nd and 3rd Squadrons of the 57th Fighter Regiment (later 57th Fighter Group), 57th Air Base "Mihail Kogălniceanu", now "Capitan Aviator Constantin Cantacuzino", near Constanta, Romania. One additional MiG-29S (9.13) was procured from the Republic of Modlova in 1992. The MiG-29 aircraft from the Romanian Air Force were operational in the period 1990-2001.

Unfortunatelly, the MiG-29 did not have a long career in the Romanian Air Force. Several factors contributed to this relatively short active period: Romania's defence budget collapsed during the 1990s, with flying hours dropping dramatically. Romania never produced any of the MiG-19's components locally, and as such, major components like the RD-33 engines were expensive and required Russian support, with spare parts becoming increasingly difficult to obtain. As Romania moved toward NATO membership, Soviet avionics and communications became increasingly obsolete. The fleet suffered several notable losses: red 41 crashed in March 1994, red 47 was destroyed by fire in 1991. At least two UB trainers were lost in accidents, one being repairable.

On May 5, 2000, the prototype/demonstrator MiG-29 Sniper (White 67, former Red 67) made its maiden flight taking off from Bacau Airport, with the test pilot of DASA/Daimler Chrysler Aerospace, Wolfgang Schirdewahn, a collaborative product of Aerostar Bacău (Romania), DASA (Germany) and Elbit Systems (Israel). The official presentation of the Sniper project took place on May 12, 2000. In the period 2000-2001, the MiG-29 Sniper demonstrator aircraft was presented at several national and international aeronautical events. Although the radar remained largely based on the original N019, almost everything around the pilot was transformed.

- colour multifunction displays
- HOTAS controls
- Western mission computer
- digital moving map
- modern INS/GPS navigation
- NATO-compatible communications
- MIL-STD-1553 data bus
- Western HUD
- hands-on-throttle-and-stick ergonomics
- digital video recorder
- modern electronic warfare suite
- compatibility with Western mission planning

In terms of physical appearance:

- Construction number: 2960532367 / 4305
- Originally delivered as a standard MiG-29A (9.12A), as Red 67
- Flew operationally with the 57th Fighter Regiment at Mihail Kogălniceanu.
- Selected in 1999 as the Sniper demonstrator.
- Repainted into the distinctive blue/grey scheme and renumbered White 67 for the programme.

The Sniper's cockpit looked far closer to an F-16 Block 50 than to a Soviet Fulcrum. Due to financial restraints, Germany retiring their own MiG-29 fleet, and the unchanged dependence on Russian RD-33 engines, the program was halted, with the MiG-29 Sniper performing the last flight during 2001. In 2009, White 67 Sniper was donated to the National Museum of Romanian Aviation.

Ironically, Romania instead kept the MiG-21 LanceR in frontline service until 2023, while the far more capable MiG-29 disappeared two decades earlier. For many Romanian aviation enthusiasts, the blue-painted White 67 Sniper has become a symbol of a lost opportunity, an opportunity that never quite came to fruition. It remains one of the most distinctive and recognisable Fulcrums ever built.
