## Grumman F-14A Tomcat BuNo 162701

**BuNo 162701** is a particularly interesting Tomcat. It had a long career, including **VF-32's CAG aircraft during Desert Storm**, and later became an F-14B with VF-143.

### Grumman F-14A-140-GR BuNo 162701

* **Manufacturer:** Grumman
* **Construction number:** **547 / KB-11**
* **Original variant:** F-14A
* **Later:** converted to **F-14B**
* **BuNo:** **162701**
* Eventually scrapped in 2008. ([Wikimedia Commons][1])

### Early career — VF-14

162701 was one of the late-production F-14As and was initially operated by **VF-14 "Tophatters"**. There's photographic evidence of it with VF-14 at **NAS Fallon in 1988**, coded **AC-106**. ([Wikimedia Commons][1]). Interestingly, AirHistory describes it as an **F-14A+** in its 1987 record. That's essentially reflecting its later engine/conversion history rather than it having been built as a different basic airframe. ([AirHistory][2])

### VF-32 — the famous period

By 1990 it was with **VF-32 "Swordsmen"**, flying as **AC-200**, the squadron CAG aircraft aboard **USS John F. Kennedy (CV-67)**. It was a very high-visibility aircraft, with the colourful VF-32 CAG markings. ([Modeling Madness][3]) And this is the period that makes 162701 famous. It went to war during **Operation Desert Storm** as **"Gypsy 200"**, the VF-32 CAG jet. It carried **two MiG-23 kill markings** on the nose. ([kitreview.com][4]) - the 4 January 1989 MiG kills, the two kills made by the other VF-32 Tomcats **159473 and 159610**. 162701 subsequently carried their kill markings as the CAG aircraft. ([Modeling Madness][3]). 

### Desert Storm appearance

**"Black 200 / Gypsy 200"**

* Gloss Light Gull Gray overall
* Very dark blue tails
* Dark blue ventral fins
* Dark blue canopy surround
* Black anti-glare panel
* Yellow AC-200/CAG markings
* Multicoloured sword motif on the rudders
* Two MiG-23 kill markings

There is some disagreement in modelling references over whether the tails were **very dark blue or actually black**, but contemporary decal research tends toward the latter interpretation. ([kitreview.com][4])

### Conversion to F-14B

Like a number of late F-14As, **162701 was subsequently converted to F-14B standard**, receiving the TF30-P-414A engines and associated modifications. This is why you'll find photographs of the same BuNo labelled both **F-14A and F-14B**.

By the mid-1990s it was with **VF-143 "Pukin' Dogs"**, coded **AG-111**. There's a particularly nice photograph of it in **May 1996 at Palma de Mallorca**, operating from **USS George Washington (CVN-73)**. ([JetPhotos][5])

So its career essentially went:

**F-14A → VF-14 → VF-32 CAG / Desert Storm → F-14B conversion → VF-143 → AMARC → scrap**

The Navy sent it to **AMARC on 17 March 2005**, still wearing VF-143's **AG-111** markings. It was struck off charge on **27 July 2005**, remained in AMARC inventory into 2008, and was finally transferred to **HVF West, Tucson, on 16 September 2008 and scrapped**. ([Aircraft Information][6])

### Tamiya connection

If you're looking at the **Tamiya 1/48 F-14A Late Model**, **162701 is actually one of the kit's marking options** — specifically the **VF-32 CAG aircraft at NAS Oceana in May 1990**. ([Manuals+][7]). So if you're building the Tamiya kit as **162701**, you can legitimately portray its **1990 VF-32 high-vis configuration**. The Desert Storm version is essentially the same famous airframe a few months later, with the wartime markings/kill markings added.
